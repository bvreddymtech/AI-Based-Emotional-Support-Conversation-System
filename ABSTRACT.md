Abstract 
Emotional distress arising from academic pressure, interpersonal difficulties, personal 
setbacks, and other everyday situations can affect an individual's well-being and ability to 
cope with challenges. Although conversational Large Language Models (LLMs) can 
generate natural and empathetic responses, producing consistently appropriate emotional 
support remains difficult because an effective response depends on understanding the user's 
emotional state, situation, conversation history, and the appropriate support strategy. 
Existing research has shown that LLMs may struggle to select suitable emotional-support 
strategies and can exhibit a preference for repeatedly using particular strategies, which may 
reduce the effectiveness of their responses. This project focuses on developing an AI-based 
conversational system that can provide context-aware emotional support to individuals 
seeking non-clinical conversational assistance. 
 
The proposed system will use publicly available emotional-support conversation data, 
particularly the Emotional Support Conversation dataset and related unsuccessful 
conversation examples, to study the relationship between user emotions, conversational 
context, support strategies, and response quality. The system will analyze the conversation 
context to identify relevant emotional information and determine suitable support strategies 
before generating a response using an LLM. Generated responses will subsequently be 
evaluated using measurable criteria such as contextual relevance, emotional appropriateness, 
support-strategy alignment, empathy, and safety. The study will compare a baseline LLM 
response generation approach with approaches incorporating emotional and strategy-related 
information to determine whether explicit strategy awareness improves response quality. 
 
The primary scope of the project is non-clinical conversational emotional support and 
research-based evaluation of AI-generated responses. The system will not attempt to 
diagnose mental-health conditions, provide medical or psychological treatment, replace 
professional counsellors, or make clinical decisions. The effectiveness of the proposed 
approach will be assessed through comparative experiments using appropriate automatic 
evaluation measures and qualitative assessment. Success will be indicated by measurable 
improvement in response quality and support-strategy alignment compared with the selected 
baseline approach. The expected outcome is an experimentally evaluated AI-based 
emotional-support conversation framework that demonstrates how contextual understanding 
and appropriate support-strategy selection can contribute to more relevant, empathetic, and 
reliable conversational responses.
